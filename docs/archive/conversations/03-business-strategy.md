# Conversation 3: Business / Strategy (20 turns)

**Goal:** Address stagnating SaaS growth and find the right path forward.

---

## Turn 1

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | Our SaaS product growth has stagnated. What should we do? |
| **C3** | Growth stagnation can have multiple root causes. Before I suggest solutions, I need to understand: is the problem on the **acquisition** side (not enough new customers), **retention** side (customers leaving), or **expansion** side (existing customers not buying more)? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `exploration`
- Action: `ask`
- Reason: "Stagnating growth" is symptom, not diagnosis

---

## Turn 2

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | We're getting fewer new signups than before. |
| **C3** | So the issue is acquisition. Is this a **demand problem** (market isn't looking for your solution) or a **conversion problem** (people visit but don't sign up)? What's happening to your website traffic and demo requests? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `exploration`
- Action: `ask`
- Reason: Narrowing down acquisition problem

---

## Turn 3

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | Traffic is actually up, but conversion is down. People aren't signing up. |
| **C3** | Interesting - that suggests your top-of-funnel is working, but something breaks during conversion. This is a different problem than "market doesn't want us." Let me understand: has something changed recently? Pricing, product, messaging, competition? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `exploration` → `clarification`
- Action: `ask`
- Goal forming: "Conversion optimization" not "growth strategy"

---

## Turn 4

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | We raised prices 6 months ago. Also, a competitor launched a free tier. |
| **C3** | **Two major changes.** I want to separate them. Which happened first, and when did you first notice the conversion drop? Timing correlation would tell us which is the likely cause. |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `clarification`
- Action: `ask`
- Checkpoint created: `Root cause identified`

---

## Turn 5

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | We raised prices in March. Competitor launched in April. Conversion started dropping in May. |
| **C3** | So conversion dropped after both changes. This makes it hard to isolate the cause. Do you have data on why people don't convert? Exit surveys, sales call objections, or support inquiries from trial users? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `clarification`
- Action: `ask`

---

## Turn 6

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | Sales says people mention price a lot. But we also hear "we're evaluating alternatives." |
| **C3** | Both signals are present. Let me frame this: you're facing a **value perception problem**. Either your price is too high for perceived value, or competition has reset what "good value" looks like. Do you know your competitor's pricing and what they're offering? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `clarification`
- Action: `ask`
- Goal refined: "Value perception problem"
- Confidence: 0.75

---

## Turn 7

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | Their free tier has most of our basic features. Their paid is cheaper too. |
| **C3** | ⚠️ **Let me pause and reframe.** The question isn't "how do we compete on price" - you likely can't win that battle. The real question is: **What do you do that they don't, and is it valuable enough to justify premium pricing?** Do you have a clear differentiation? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `clarification`
- Action: `reframe`
- Reason: Preventing "race to bottom" thinking

---

## Turn 8

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | We have better analytics and integrations. Enterprise customers love us. |
| **C3** | That's significant. Enterprise customers love you, but you're competing for SMB signups against a cheaper player. This suggests a **positioning problem**, not a pricing problem. You might be targeting the wrong segment. What percentage of your revenue comes from enterprise vs SMB? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `clarification`
- Action: `ask`
- Goal evolution: "Positioning problem" suspected

---

## Turn 9

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | About 70% enterprise, 30% SMB. |
| **C3** | **Critical insight:** 70% of revenue from enterprise, but you're worried about SMB signup conversion? Let me challenge this: if you could grow enterprise 20% instead of chasing SMB signups, would that solve your growth problem? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `clarification`
- Action: `challenge`
- Reason: User may be solving the wrong problem
- Checkpoint created: `Problem vs symptom distinguished`

---

## Turn 10

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | Actually... probably yes. Enterprise deals are much larger. |
| **C3** | So here's the real question: **Should you compete for SMB against a free competitor, or double down on enterprise where you're already winning?** This is a strategic choice. What do you believe? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `clarification`
- Action: `ask`
- Reason: Forcing strategic decision before solutioning

---

## Turn 11

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | I think we should focus on enterprise. The SMB signups mostly churn anyway. |
| **C3** | **Checkpoint confirmed:** Primary goal is enterprise growth, not SMB conversion. This completely changes the strategy. The stagnating "signups" were a symptom - the real issue is you need more enterprise pipeline. |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `clarification` → `solutioning`
- Action: `answer`
- Goal locked: "Enterprise growth strategy"
- Checkpoint satisfied: `Problem vs symptom distinguished`

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `solutioning`
- Action: `ask`
- Reason: Assessing current capabilities

Can you afford to hire? 2. **ABM program** - Target 100 ideal companies specifically 3. **Integration partnerships** - Use partner channels for reach 4. **Case studies** - You have these, but are they being used effectively in sales? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `solutioning`
- Action: `answer`

## Turn 19

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | I think so. What about our pricing - do we need to change it? |
| **C3** | For enterprise, your pricing may actually be **too low**, not too high. Enterprise buyers expect premium pricing and may distrust "cheap" solutions. Consider: (1) Keep current pricing as your anchor, (2) Add premium tier with white-glove support, (3) Annual contracts with discounts for commitment. Don't lower prices to compete with free. |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `solutioning`
- Action: `answer`
- Checkpoint created: `Pricing strategy confirmed`

## Turn 20

| Role | Content |
|------|---------|
| **USER** | This is really different from what I thought we needed to do. |
| **C3** | **That's the reframe.** You came in thinking "fix signup conversion." The real strategy is: 1. **Stop chasing SMB** - let the competitor have that segment 2. **Double down on enterprise** - where you already win 3. **ABM pilot** - 100 target companies, 90-day experiment 4. **Grow sales capacity** - 1-2 new hires 5. **Consider premium pricing** - not discounting **Original problem:** "Signups are down" **Actual problem:** "We were optimizing the wrong segment" Want me to create a 90-day action plan with milestones? |

**D6 Strategy:**
- Phase: `validation` → `closure`
- Action: `summarize`
- Reason: Conversation complete, offering next step

**Final Checkpoints:**
- ✅ Root cause identified (positioning, not pricing)
- ✅ Problem vs symptom distinguished (SMB signups ≠ growth problem)
- ✅ Success metrics defined (5 qualified meetings from 100 targets)
- ✅ ICP defined (fintech/ecomm, 50-500 emp, funded)
- ✅ Pricing strategy confirmed (premium, not discount)

## D6 Effect Summary

Without D6:
- Would have answered "how to improve signup conversion" directly
- Might have suggested pricing cuts or free tier
- Endless tactical suggestions without strategic reframe

With D6:
- Discovered the real problem at turn 9 (70% enterprise revenue)
- Forced strategic decision at turn 10 (SMB vs enterprise)
- Locked goal before solutioning at turn 11
- Built structured 90-day plan
- Clear reframe visible to user at turn 20
